\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\vspace{-0.08cm}
We introduced \textbf{\texttt{fin-skills}} alongside an external counterfactual and microstructure audit methodology for financial AI agents. Combining black-box perturbations, multi-round guard self-repair ($98.3\%$ \texttt{Pass@3} vs.\ $71.7\%$ Python \texttt{Self-Debug}), and bilingual Bayesian KOL calibration ($2{,}521$ KOLs) eliminates backtest shortcut illusions and bridges financial NLP with live execution.

\clearpage

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We explicitly bound the operating regime and failure boundaries of \texttt{fin-skills}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Verification vs.\ Alpha Generation}: As shown in \S\ref{sec:pilot}, \texttt{fin-skills} is an integrity verification toolkit rather than an alpha generator: when underlying strategies lack edge, guards prevent false discoveries without inflating realized returns.
    \item \textbf{Residual Edge Cases}: On \texttt{FinGuardBench-60}, $1/60$ ($1.7\%$) of tasks (\texttt{T60}, nested multi-artifact provenance reconciliation) remained unrepaired at \texttt{Pass@3} ($98.3\%$) on the open-coder tier, requiring frontier reasoning models ($100.0\%$) or human review.
    \item \textbf{Platform Drift}: Social recommendation algorithms evolve over time, requiring periodic rolling re-estimation of Bayesian KOL priors to prevent structural drift.
\end{enumerate}
